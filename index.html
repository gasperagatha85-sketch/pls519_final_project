We do not live to survive

Who Kazakhstan's safety net doesn't see

More than a million people in Kazakhstan live below the subsistence minimum. Most get no targeted social assistance (TSA). This project asks how AI can help poor and vulnerable families reach the support that already exists.

receive TSA: about 167,000 peoplepoor, no TSA: about 850,000

Each figure is about 50,000 people below the subsistence minimum (1,021,469 in 2025, Bureau of National Statistics). TSA recipients: 167,400 on 1 April 2026 (Ministry of Labour). The two numbers come from different sources and dates, so treat this as a rough picture of scale.

1. The problem

Poor by the state's measure, but not poor enough for its help

Poverty fell from 18.2% in 2006 to 5.2% in 2023, then stopped at 5%. In 2025 GDP per person grew by 5.2%, yet real wages fell by 1.4% and incomes by 2.3%. Growth did not reach the poor.

Why the gap exists: two lines, not one

Poverty is measured against each region's subsistence minimum, updated every quarter. TSA uses a lower line: in practice 70% of the previous quarter's minimum (Order No. 473). A family between the two lines is poor by the state's own measure, but not "poor enough" for its help.

2. Background and data

Official data show who is poor, not who is about to be

The Bureau of National Statistics measures poverty from a quarterly survey of 12,000 households. Income is counted per equivalent adult: the first member counts as 1, each further member as 0.8. TSA counts come from a different source, the Ministry's administrative records.

What the data cannot see

No panel dataNo family is followed over time, so no one sees who is one shock away.
A sample, not a register12,000 households give regional rates but can't locate families.
Two sources that don't meetPoverty comes from a survey, TSA from Ministry records, so the gap between them cannot be measured.
TSA coverage unknownThe national total is published, but not how many poor people, or which regions, receive TSA.
Poor data qualityThe state's own AI Concept 2024–2029 admits gaps and duplication.

How poor are the poor?

The poverty rate counts how many people are poor. Poverty depth shows how poor they are: the average shortfall of incomes below the minimum, as a share of the minimum, counted across the whole population, with people above the line counting as zero (Bureau of National Statistics). Nationally the average poor person is about 16% short (0.8 ÷ 5.0), and closing the whole gap would cost about 145 bn ₸ a year.

3. Current state of research

We know who is poor. We don't know who is about to be, or who is missed.

  • A
    Poverty dynamics in KazakhstanUp to a quarter of people were persistently poor in 2001–2009, and families with children under six fell in more and climbed out less (Kudebayeva 2018, 2019; Kudebayeva & Barrientos 2017). No panel data after 2009.
  • B
    Vulnerability: who is about to become poorRisk of poverty can be estimated from one survey (Chaudhuri, Jalan & Suryahadi 2002), as was done for Kazakhstan in the 2008–09 crisis (Kudebayeva 2012). Help before a shock works better than after (Pople et al. 2026). Not measured for today's near-poor.
  • C
    Targeting and exclusionTargeted programmes miss many poor people (Brown, Ravallion & van de Walle 2018; Kidd et al. 2017), in Kyrgyzstan (OECD 2018) and Uzbekistan (World Bank 2019) too. Whether targeting or universal transfers do better is disputed (Hanna & Olken 2018). Kazakhstan's two-line gap is unstudied.

The gap: nobody measures who is one shock from poverty, targeted help misses many of the poor, and no AI tool has been tested to help families in Kazakhstan find what they can claim.

4. The AI-assisted solution

One system, two doors: the state looks ahead, the family asks

The state's door

Shock screen

  1. AI reads this year's statistics
  2. Proposes the shocks to test
  3. Analysts approve the list
  4. Families one shock from poverty get an offer of a visit

Helps before a family becomes poor

The family's door

AI adviser

  1. Family opens a chat in Kazakh or Russian
  2. Answers a few questions
  3. Gets the programmes it can apply for
  4. Plus documents and an appeal draft

Helps once it is, even if the state can't see its income

Behind both doors: the same list of real programmes. Neither door grants, cuts or refuses aid.

    Where AI advisers go wrong

    US, AI chatbots for benefits

    Language models often get eligibility rules wrong (Toles et al. 2025). When a chatbot is wrong, caseworkers using it make more mistakes (Gosciak et al. 2026). People may trust AI too much and face new paperwork (Jo et al. 2025).

    So: the adviser answers only from the official list, shows its source, and hands over to a person. These studies are from the US; none tested Kazakh or Russian.

    Lessons taken: a social worker decides and every family is told why (Nigeria); a person stands behind the technology (India); the adviser helps new social workers most (US); an adviser must show its sources, because wrong answers mislead (US chatbot studies). Unlike Jordan's Takaful, nothing here decides payments.

    4. What it would cost

    The software is cheap. Everything around it is not.

    Environmental
    Low–med

    The screen is arithmetic. The adviser runs a language model per chat, on a grid that is 62% coal (604 g CO₂/kWh).

    Economic
    High

    The deciding cost. More families will ask for help: closing the poverty gap costs ~145 bn ₸ a year, 2.3× the TSA budget, while the 2027 reform aims to save 700 bn.

    Labor
    Medium

    Analysts check the AI's shocks; 12,000 short-staffed social workers do the visits; people must back the chat, since information alone helps mainly the literate (Gupta 2026).

    Governance
    High

    Registry data needs a legal basis (AI Law Art. 17(3)(3)). High-risk under the EU AI Act (Annex III 5(a)); users must know it's an AI (Art. 50). Welfare data can serve control (Earl, Maher & Pan 2022).

    Data ethics
    Medium

    No consent to the screen's use of state data; chats must store nothing. When systems fail, the unprotected "are left to carry the pieces" (Mhlambi 2020).

    The 145 bn ₸ poverty gap is the author's calculation from the Bureau's 2025 poverty depth; assumptions are in the research paper.

    Verdict

    Yes, under conditions

    AI adviser: yes, first

    • Answers only from the official list, with sources, and says it is an AI
    • Stores nothing without consent; shares nothing with the state
    • Tested in Kazakh and Russian; a person reachable

    Shock screen: yes, after a law and a pilot

    • A law, not only a ministerial order, allows this use of registry data (AI Law Art. 17(3)(3))
    • AI proposes shocks; analysts approve and publish
    • A flag only adds a visit; a social worker decides (Art. 9, 17(2)(1))
    • Families told why, can refuse or appeal (Art. 7(2))
    • Errors audited and published (Art. 6)
    • Social-worker posts and local programmes funded

    If the money for help is not there, the answer is no.

    Both doors bring more families to ask for help. Paying everyone below the line would cost about 145 bn ₸ a year, 2.3× today's TSA budget, while the 2027 reform aims to save 700 bn ₸. Promises without money break trust: in Nigeria, some families were cut without a reason, and some borrowed money expecting aid that never came.

    Nothing here has been tested on real families, so it starts small:

    1. Pilotdistricts in Ulytau, Mangystau or Abai, the regions with the deepest poverty
    2. Comparewith similar districts that start a year later
    3. Scaleonly if take-up and incomes rise and the poverty gap falls
